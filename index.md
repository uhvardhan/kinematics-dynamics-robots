Welcome to Kinematics and Dynamics of Robots (25PC1RA204).

This course teaches the mathematics that describes and predicts the motion of robot manipulators. The course has five units:

- **Rigid Body Foundations and Spatial Analysis.** Vectors, rotation matrices, and homogeneous transformations represent points, frames, and rigid bodies in space.
- **Forward Kinematics (D-H Convention).** Forward kinematics calculates the position and orientation of the end effector from the joint values. The Denavit–Hartenberg convention assigns a frame and four parameters to each link of a serial robot. The unit applies this method to articulated, SCARA, and cylindrical robots.
- **Inverse Kinematics.** Inverse kinematics finds the joint values for a given position and orientation of the end effector. The unit compares algebraic and geometric solutions. It also examines the workspace, solvability, and dexterity of a robot.
- **Differential Motions and Jacobians.** The Jacobian matrix relates the joint velocities to the velocity of the end effector. Singularity analysis finds the positions where the robot loses one or more directions of motion.
- **Dynamic Analysis and Force Analysis.** Lagrangian mechanics gives the equations of motion for robots with more than one degree of freedom. Static force analysis relates the joint torques to the forces and moments at the end effector.
